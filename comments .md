Comment 2:
In this paper, the author presents two main components, both centered on the application of tendon-driven mechanisms. The
first section focuses on the design of a medical instrument, which appears to be a handheld device intended for use in
laparoscopic surgery. The motivation for developing a compact and ergonomically suitable device is reasonable and aligns
well with the constraints typically encountered in minimally invasive surgical procedures. The general concept of
miniaturization and improved maneuverability is clearly conveyed, and the proposed design appears to be intuitive from a
functional standpoint.

However, the teclimcaljustification for the design choices is insufficient. In particular, the paper does not include any finite element analysis (FEA) to validate the structural integrity of the proposed mechanism or to assess stress distribution under expected operating conditions. Furthennore, the selection of the motors driving the tendon system is not supported by quantitative analysis. Without FEA results, it is difficult to evaluate whether the device can reliably withstand operational loads or whether the chosen actuators are appropriately sized. Including such analyses would s ignificantly strengthen the credibility of the design and provide objective support for the engineering decisions presented.

In the second part of the paper, the focus shifts to a robotic gripper, specifically Lasso Gripper. Although both sections
involve tendon-driven mechanisms, the connection between the two sections is weak and insufficiently developed. The
transition between these topics appears abrupt, and the paper does not adequately explain how the second system builds upon, extends, or is informed by the first. The author should more explicitly articulate the conceptual or methodological
relationship between these two components to improve the overall coherence and continuity of the paper. Additionally, the experimental setup for Lasso Gripper lacks clarity. The paper provides limited explanation of how the
gripper determines when to initiate grasping or release actions. No internal or external sensing mechanisms are described,
making it unclear how state transitions are controlled during operation. A detailed diagram or flowchart illustrating the
complete grasping and releasing process, including actuation logic and control signals, would greatly enhance the reader's
understanding.

Finally, the paper does not report any quantitative evaluation of experimental perfonnance, such as success rates,
repeatability, or failure cases. NOT does it provide a rationale for the absence of such metrics. Including experimental results or explaining their omission is essential for assessing the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed gripper design.
